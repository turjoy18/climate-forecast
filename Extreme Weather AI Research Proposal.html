Forecast the tail, not the mean.

The submitted draft proposes an LSTM/CNN, a Flask API and a React Native app. That project would be obsolete on arrival: deterministic AI models already beat ECMWF IFS HRES on most average scores — and systematically fail exactly where this proposal claims to work, on record-breaking extremes and tropical-cyclone intensity. This rebuild is a novel-ML-method programme with climate as the stress domain: extremes-weighted + twCRPS LoRA / full fine-tune of Aurora on a Western Pacific crop, with IBTrACS-supervised intensity objectives and a secondary 0–6 h heavy-rain arm — evaluated on WeatherBench 2 + CSI/ETS/FSS/twCRPS/TC metrics against observations. No React Native app. No diffusion model trained from scratch. No quantum.

The proposed project, in one paragraph

Working title: Tail-First Fine-Tuning: Extremes-Weighted LoRA Adaptation of Aurora for Western Pacific Tropical-Cyclone Intensity and Heavy Precipitation.

Domain: Western Pacific crop, 100–135°E, 5–40°N — HK / SE Asia. Core: 0–72 h TC intensity (+ track as secondary) and heavy-precip exceedance. Secondary: 0–6 h nowcasting / black-rainstorm (>70 mm/h) arm. Loss: extremes-weighted + quantile + twCRPS. Base: Aurora-Small (113M, full FT) and/or Aurora 1.3B (LoRA). Budget: ~5–20 GPU-days for the core result. Deliverable: arXiv draft + code/repro + evaluation notebooks.

The honest verdict on the draft

Kill the app. Keep the ambition.

The prototype app, usability testing and “which features matter?” question are undergraduate deliverables. No venue in 2026 publishes them. The scientific deliverable — a calibrated extremes forecast that beats HRES where HRES currently wins — is harder, smaller in scope, and worth a paper. Visualisation returns at the end, as the paper's figures and a decision-value analysis, not as a product.

02

The evidence: AI wins the mean, loses the tail

Three findings define the problem. Together they are the introduction of the paper — and the reason “another deterministic model” is not a contribution.

Finding 1 · Record-breaking events
~249,000 records, 2020

HRES beats the AI models on records

Zhang, Fischer, Zscheischler & Engelke (Science Advances, 2026; doi:10.1126/sciadv.aec1433) show HRES still consistently outperforms GraphCast, Pangu-Weather and FuXi on record-breaking heat, cold and wind across nearly all lead times: AI models under-estimate frequency and intensity, with growing error for larger record exceedance. On ordinary cases, AI wins. Exact event counts: confirm from the journal PDF before citing a number.

Finding 2 · Gray-swan cyclones
980 vs 960 hPa

Unseen intensity is not learned from the mean

Sun et al. (PNAS, May 2025) retrained FourCastNet with all Category 3–5 cyclones (1979–2015) removed. Tested on 20 Category-5 storms (2018–23), it failed to forecast intensification — Hurricane Lee's minimum pressure never fell below 980 hPa against a 960 hPa reference — and its wind/pressure fields violate gradient-wind balance. Mean-RMSE tuning cannot fix unseen-tail physics.

Finding 3 · Precipitation smoothing
20–35% under-forecast

MSE training shrinks the peaks it should predict

Deterministic MSE-trained models underestimate 99th-percentile precipitation by roughly 20–35% (range as reported in secondary summaries of Hamill et al., GRL 2024, and others — attribute as a reported range, not a directly verified figure). In Bangladesh (2023–24, 43 stations), GraphCast's extreme-rain CSI was only 0.45–0.52 with false-alarm ratio 0.48–0.55 at 100–300 mm, despite routine-rain POD above 0.98.

The scale trap. Loveday & Hertneky (Geosci. Model Dev., 2026; 973 initialisations, CONUS gauges) show the winner on precipitation depends on scale: smooth AI forecasts win point-scale threshold-weighted CRPS, while the 3 km HRRR wins at neighbourhood scale, especially at 6 h and the 99.9th percentile. Any extremes claim that reports only point RMSE has chosen the metric that flatters it. This proposal reports neighbourhood scores (FSS) as primary.

04

Research questions & programme

The draft's three questions — which features matter, can deep learning beat traditional models, how to visualise for non-experts — are unanswerable as posed (the first is a feature-importance exercise, the second was settled in 2023, the third is not a research question). They are replaced by three questions that can fail.

RQ1

Can extremes-weighted LoRA adaptation recover tail skill that the foundation model lost to mean training?

Fine-tune Aurora (LoRA on backbone self-attention, the recipe in the Nature supplement §D.4) and Aurora-Small (full fine-tune, 113M params) on extreme-subsampled Western Pacific cases with an extremes-weighted + quantile loss. Measure record-exceedance error, CSI/ETS and threshold-weighted CRPS against the frozen base model.

Falsified if: tail metrics do not improve over frozen Aurora and frozen+Gomez at matched body metrics (mean RMSE / unweighted CRPS), or improve only by degrading calibration. Always report body + tail (Wessel body–tail trade-off).

RQ2

Can IBTrACS-supervised intensity objectives close the TC intensity gap without training a diffusion model from scratch?

Track is largely solved by Aurora / GenCast; intensity is the known failure mode (reanalysis-smoothed extrema, MSE attenuation). Add multi-task intensity heads and/or tracking-independent postprocessing (Gomez et al., AIES 2026) on Aurora fields, trained against IBTrACS max wind / min pressure over held-out WNP seasons — and report against HRES and FuXi-TC as the generative upper bar we are not reproducing.

Falsified if: intensity error does not move toward HRES on held-out storms at matched track error after the frozen-Aurora + Gomez-head control, or gains come only from memorising storm IDs. Gomez used Pangu/FourCastNet — Aurora transfer is itself a test.

RQ3

Is a LoRA-ensemble calibrated where it matters — and worth anything to a decision-maker?

Build a 10–50 member ensemble via LoRA diversity / IC perturbation / MC-dropout-class methods (not diffusion sampling as the core — that is stretch-only). Report rank histograms, spread–skill and reliability as primary results, plus Relative Economic Value across cost/loss ratios — the metric GenCast uses to connect skill to use.

Falsified if: spread–skill deviates materially from 1 on extremes thresholds after CRPS/twCRPS fine-tuning and temperature scaling.

05

Evaluation: the baseline bar

WeatherBench 2 (Rasp et al., JAMES 2024) is the protocol floor — 2020 evaluation year, 00/12 UTC initialisations, ERA5 truth, HRES deterministic and ENS probabilistic baselines — but its core protocol contains no CSI, ETS, FSS, threshold or cyclone metrics. An extremes paper must add them, plus the Zhao et al. binary-extremes extension (Geosci. Model Dev. 18:5781–5799, 2025; ROC skill score recommended as rarity-insensitive) and, for km-scale claims, HR-Extreme (ICLR 2025, from NOAA HRRR 3 km data), where SOTA models' extreme-case errors are significantly larger than their overall errors.

CSI / ETS · POD / FAR

Binary exceedance at multiple thresholds (e.g. rain >50 / 100 mm, T >95th percentile). CSI collapses for rare events — always pair with ETS / ROCSS.

FSS (neighbourhood)

Fractions Skill Score at several neighbourhood sizes. Essential for convective rain — the double-penalty problem punishes sharp, slightly displaced forecasts.

twCRPS (+ body)

Threshold-weighted CRPS for the tail. Wessel et al. (MWR 2025): twCRPS training improves extremes but can worsen the body — always report unweighted CRPS / mean RMSE alongside, and mitigate with weighted training or linear pooling.

Quantile / pinball score

Error at the 90th / 95th / 99th percentiles, reported per quantile — the direct antidote to MSE's regression to the mean. Never collapse to one weighted number.

TC track + intensity

Track error (km), minimum-pressure and maximum-wind error, rapid-intensification detection — against IBTrACS, not ERA5.

Calibration + REV

Rank histogram, spread–skill ratio (≈1 = calibrated), reliability diagrams, and Relative Economic Value over cost/loss ratios. An uncalibrated ensemble is not a result.

A credible paper must beat all five, on the same protocol

  • 1
    Persistence and climatology

    Trivial, but required — many “AI beats NWP” extremes claims never show it.

  • 2
    IFS HRES (deterministic)

    For extremes, HRES is currently the model to beat (Finding 1). Beating it on mean RMSE is not sufficient.

  • 3
    IFS ENS / AIFS-ENS (probabilistic)

    The CRPS bar GenCast set: better than ENS on 97.2% of 1,320 targets.

  • 4
    A released AI model on the same task

    GraphCast (deterministic), GenCast or AIFS-ENS (probabilistic), Aurora (foundation); FuXi-Extreme / StormCast / HRRR for the regional arm.

  • 5
    Observations, held-out year, significance

    Stations / IMERG / IBTrACS verification, a test year the model never trained on, paired tests or bootstrap — not a single case study. Storm Ciarán and Hurricane Lee are illustrations, not proof.

Stretch goal — compound extremes (WP3, high risk / high reward). There is no standard compound-event benchmark for ML weather models as of 2026: no baseline, no agreed metric. That absence is the opportunity. A tractable first definition: joint exceedance of temperature and precipitation above their 95th percentiles in the same 3-day window, or cyclone rain falling on antecedently saturated catchments, evaluated with joint Brier score and multivariate CRPS (energy score). Definitions follow Tang et al. (2025, China compound review) and Raymond et al. (Nat. Rev. Earth Environ., 2026). Do not claim to “solve” compounds with a univariate model.

The recommended configuration

Base: Aurora-Small (full FT) and/or Aurora 1.3B (LoRA). Domain: 100–135°E, 5–40°N. Task: 0–72 h TC intensity (+ track) and heavy-precip exceedance; 0–6 h secondary arm. Physics, honestly scoped: extremes-weighted, quantile, CRPS and spectral losses are cheap and demonstrated. Soft constraints (gradient-wind balance, water balance, motion-regularised advection) are credible. A hard PINN with PDE-residual loss on the full atmosphere has not worked at global scale — do not propose one. NeuralGCM's differentiable-core hybrid is the credible physics route, and it is cluster-scale: cite it as inspiration, not as a plan.

07

Quantum: the blunt section

The brief invited “very advanced machine learning and quantum computing stuff.” The research answer, applied to this project, is no — and a proposal that says so, with reasons, is stronger than one that bolts quantum on.

Verdict: mostly bolted-on. Zero operational use. Not the core of this thesis.

As of 23 Oct 2026, zero operational weather forecasts run on a quantum computer anywhere, under Rosetta Quantum's three criteria (operational schedule, quantum processor in the loop, publicly documented). Tennie & Palmer (Oxford, BAMS 2023) give the structural reasons:

  • Readout bottleneck: a 10⁹-variable atmospheric state fits in ~30 qubits, but measurement returns ~1 bit per qubit — a forecast centre needs the whole field back, every cycle. They call this insurmountable for operational centres.
  • Nonlinearity: the Schrödinger equation is linear; the atmosphere is not, and chaotic systems may need exponentially many copies to track.
  • Scale: inverting one forecast-scale linear system was estimated at ~10²⁹ gates (Scherer et al., 2017). No published shortcut has removed this as of Oct 2026.

What exists in 2024–2026 is toy-scale: QNN demos on single variables, simulator-only, with no IFS / GraphCast / Aurora comparison and no extremes evaluation (e.g. arXiv:2509.01422). Ueno & Miura's quantum stochastic-parameterisation algorithm needed ~4× more samples than classical Monte Carlo for the same result, on a simulator. The CERN Open Quantum Institute weather pilot is at “Phase 2 — Full Proposal”: no runs, no results.

The only honest quantum-adjacent option: treat tensor networks as what they are — a classical compression method — and benchmark them against a classical baseline on one small, well-defined subgrid task (cloud cover or radiation parameterisation), prepared for a negative result. Or run a QNN as a negative control. Do not claim quantum advantage, and do not spend Blackwell hours simulating a global quantum forecast. Your existing quantum portfolio (circuit scheduling, ML entanglement detection, fair entanglement routing) already proves quantum competence; this paper proves scientific judgement by not forcing it.
